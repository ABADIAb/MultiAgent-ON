\chapter{Experimental Evaluation and Results}
\label{chap:evaluation}

This chapter evaluates the Risk-Adaptive Decision Gate (RADG) architecture introduced in Chapter~\ref{chap:system_model} and implemented in Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation}. The evaluation quantifies the system's ability to intercept physically infeasible or semantically ambiguous intents \emph{before} they reach the SDON controller, while minimizing unnecessary operator interventions. Three baseline architectures are compared across a four-pillar validation framework using three LLMs of varying capacity evaluated over a 120-demand benchmark corpus. Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup} describes the simulated physical testbed, benchmark corpus, baselines, and evaluation metrics. Section~\ref{sec:intra_model} presents an intra-model comparative analysis using a representative cloud-hosted LLM to examine the operational dilemmas that motivate the RADG architecture. Section~\ref{sec:cross_model} examines the sensitivity of gate decisions and computational efficiency across three distinct LLM backends. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses architectural levers, limitations, and threats to validity, and Section~\ref{sec:eval_summary} consolidates the experimental findings.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Network Topology and Physical Parameters}
\label{sec:topology}

All experiments use the simulated 17-node German backbone topology with 26 bidirectional links derived from the SNDlib repository~\cite{orlowski_sndlib_2010}, depicted in Figure~\ref{fig:17-node-german}. The network models a national optical core network where link spans range from 37.5\,km to 381.9\,km, reflecting realistic metropolitan and long-haul inter-city fiber segments. The physical layer is parameterized for standard ITU-T G.652 single-mode fiber (SMF-28) with attenuation coefficient $\alpha = 0.25$\,dB/km, nonlinear Kerr coefficient $\gamma = 1.27$\,W$^{-1}$km$^{-1}$, and group velocity dispersion parameter $|\beta_2| = 21.7\times 10^{-27}$\,s$^2$/m (corresponding to chromatic dispersion $D \approx 17.0$\,ps/(nm$\cdot$km) at reference wavelength $\lambda = 1550$\,nm).

Each bidirectional link incorporates an optical amplifier chain comprising an ingress pre-amplifier, intermediate inline amplifiers (ILAs) deployed approximately every 70\,km, and an egress booster amplifier. EDFA noise figures follow gain-dependent polynomial models: boosters and ILAs operate with gains in $[10, 23]$\,dB and noise figures in $[6, 12]$\,dB, while pre-amplifiers operate with gains in $[18, 32]$\,dB and noise figures in $[6.2, 10.5]$\,dB.

\begin{figure}[!htbp]
  \centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.55\textwidth]{Figures/figs_Evaluation/17_node_german.png}
  \caption{The 17-node German core optical backbone topology simulated for experimental evaluation. Node identifiers correspond to major German metropolitan switching centers.}
  \label{fig:17-node-german}
\end{figure}
\FloatBarrier

The GN-model physics engine, detailed in Section~\ref{sec:physical_engine}, calculates accumulated Amplified Spontaneous Emission (ASE) noise and Nonlinear Interference (NLI) power across candidate lightpaths. The engine computes per-path Generalized Signal-to-Noise Ratio (GSNR) values including a 26\,dB transponder back-to-back SNR floor and evaluates receiver optical power against modulation-dependent thresholds (e.g., $\text{GSNR}_{th} = 8.6$\,dB for 100G QPSK and $15.2$\,dB for 200G 16-QAM) and receiver sensitivity bounds $P_{rx} \in [-18.0, -8.0]$\,dBm.

\subsection{LLMs Under Evaluation}
\label{sec:llm_backends}

To assess whether the neurosymbolic pipeline maintains its operational guarantees independently of model capacity, the architecture is evaluated across three distinct LLM backends:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Qwen 2.5 3B} (\texttt{qwen2.5:3b}, Ollama, local inference): A 3-billion-parameter open-weights Small Language Model (SLM) executing locally on commodity workstation hardware (NVIDIA RTX~3050, 4\,GB VRAM). This model represents the resource-constrained edge boundary for private on-premises telecommunication operations.
    \item \textbf{GPT-6 Luna} (\texttt{gpt-6-luna}, OpenAI API): A commercial cloud-hosted model representing the state of the art in reasoning depth, instruction adherence, and structured JSON output generation.
    \item \textbf{GPT-5 Nano} (\texttt{gpt-5-nano-2025-08-07}, OpenAI API): A lightweight cloud-hosted commercial model optimized for high throughput and reduced token latency at an intermediate reasoning capacity.
\end{itemize}

Crucially, all three LLM backends are evaluated against the complete 120-demand benchmark corpus, providing an identical statistical baseline for cross-model comparative analysis.

\subsection{Benchmark Corpus and Diurnal Risk Classes}
\label{sec:corpus}

The evaluation corpus models a 24-hour diurnal operational shift in a carrier-grade core optical network rather than a synthetic homogeneous batch. In production environments, routine well-formed provisioning requests arrive alongside underspecified, physically infeasible, or corrupted requests. The benchmark corpus comprises 120 operator intent demands partitioned symmetrically into four risk classes of 30 demands each:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Class~I (Nominal):} Unambiguous, physically feasible provisioning requests with complete topological constraint specifications (e.g., ``Provision a 100\,Gbps optical path from Hamburg to Munich avoiding Frankfurt''). Expected action: autonomous approval with zero Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) interrupts.
    \item \textbf{Class~II (Ambiguous):} Demands with underspecified endpoints, missing bandwidth requirements, or colloquial SLA terms that inflate semantic uncertainty $U_{sem} > \tau_{sem}$. Expected action: the Semantic RADG triggers a Clarify interrupt via reverse-prompting reconstruction.
    \item \textbf{Class~III (Physically Infeasible):} Semantically valid requests requiring lightpaths that violate GN-model physical reach (e.g., demanding high-order modulation formats on unamplified 380\,km paths yielding $\text{GSNR} < \text{GSNR}_{th}$). Expected action: the Physical RADG triggers a Replan interrupt after GN-model feasibility verification confirms $\text{QoT}_{valid} = 0$.
    \item \textbf{Class~IV (Adversarial):} Demands featuring conflicting topological constraints (e.g., requiring node inclusion and avoidance simultaneously) or nonexistent switching centers. Expected action: fail-fast pre-deployment interception at the earliest applicable decision gate.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Baseline Architectures}
\label{sec:baselines}

To isolate the contributions of the sequential decision gates, three baseline architectures are compared over the identical LangGraph pipeline, topological knowledge graph, and GN-model physics calculator:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Proposed RADG:} The full dual-gate architecture. The Semantic RADG evaluates $U_{sem}$ and the Physical RADG evaluates $\text{QoT}_{valid}$. Operator interrupts are triggered selectively, strictly proportional to assessed semantic or physical risk.
    \item \textbf{Always-On HITL:} An operational ablation where every intent, regardless of risk class, undergoes mandatory human review following PDDL parsing. This baseline isolates the human cognitive fatigue and latency overhead of indiscriminate human validation on nominal traffic.
    \item \textbf{LLM-Only:} An un-gated ablation where both decision gates are disabled. Natural language intents are parsed and forwarded directly to the SDON controller admission stage without pre-deployment semantic or physical validation. To model real-world deployment failures, un-gated invalid configurations trigger mock controller rejection payloads grounded in RFC~8040 (RESTCONF Protocol) Section~7.1.1 error response specifications~\cite{bierman_restconf_2017} and ONF TAPI conventions, forcing the LLM into a multi-turn reactive recovery loop.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Performance Metrics: The Four Validation Pillars}
\label{sec:metrics}

The evaluation framework assesses the system across four orthogonal validation pillars:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pillar~1 --- Semantic Translation Accuracy:} Measures the structural and conceptual fidelity of the neural translation:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Constraint Retention Rate ($\text{CRR}$): fraction of explicit operator constraints preserved in the PDDL AST ($\sum \|\mathcal{C}_{pres} \cap \mathcal{C}_{exp}\| / \sum \|\mathcal{C}_{exp}\|$).
        \item Context-Free Grammar Pass Rate ($\text{CFG-PR}$): fraction of PDDL outputs satisfying AST grammar rules ($v_{struct} \in \{0, 1\}$).
        \item Semantic Agreement Score: $1 - d_{sem} \in [0, 1]$, measuring neural concordance between the original intent and the reverse-prompted reconstruction.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Pillar~2 --- Physical Feasibility and Transmission Integrity:} Quantifies the pre-deployment integrity barrier protecting the physical network:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item False Positive Rate ($\text{FPR}$): percentage of non-nominal demands (Classes~II, III, IV) erroneously forwarded to controller admission ($\|\text{Risky Approved}\| / \|\text{Risky Demands}\| \times 100\%$). Target: $0.0\%$.
        \item Physical Infeasibility Interception Rate ($\text{PIIR}$): percentage of reach-violating Class~III intents intercepted before controller provisioning ($\|\text{Class III Intercepted}\| / \|\text{Class III Total}\| \times 100\%$).
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Pillar~3 --- Efficiency and Operator Friction:} Evaluates computational overhead and human cognitive burden:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item End-to-End Latency ($T_{E2E}$): turnaround duration in seconds, reported as median and mean to identify tail-latency skew.
        \item Token Footprint ($T_{tokens}$): prompt and completion token consumption across all execution turns.
        \item Mean HITL Interventions ($\bar{N}_{hitl}$): average operator interrupts triggered per intent.
        \item Task Completion Rate ($\text{TCR}$): fraction of demands executed without timeout or state exhaustion.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Pillar~4 --- Gate Reliability and Autonomy:} Evaluates decision fidelity across the multi-class intent space:
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Gate Decision Accuracy ($\text{GDA}$): percentage of initial gate actions matching ground truth ($\sum \mathbb{I}(D(U_{sem}, \text{QoT}) = \text{Action}_{GT}) / N \times 100\%$).
        \item Selective HITL Precision: proportion of triggered operator interrupts directed at genuinely non-nominal intents ($\|\text{Non-Nominal Interrupted}\| / \|\text{Total Interrupted}\| \times 100\%$).
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{The Alert Fatigue Dilemma: Why Always-On HITL Fails}
\label{sec:alert_fatigue}

The Always-On HITL baseline enforces a blanket verification policy: every intent, including routine nominal requests, requires explicit operator sign-off before symbolic path computation. Furthermore, nominal human verification introduces substantial computational overhead. Processing the redundant confirmation turns consumes an additional 139,502 tokens ($+15.0\%$) and increases mean end-to-end turnaround latency from 14.31\,s to 16.77\,s ($+17.2\%$).

\subsection{The Post-Deployment Error Dilemma: Why LLM-Only Fails}
\label{sec:controller_collapse}

The LLM-Only baseline models a conventional un-gated planner: natural language intents are translated directly into PDDL and dispatched to controller admission without pre-deployment semantic validation or physical reach calculation. Table~\ref{tab:controller_collapse} summarizes the resulting transmission integrity and efficiency impact.

\centering

\label{tab:controller_collapse}
\begin{tabular}{lcc}

Without pre-deployment decision gates, all 120 demands are forwarded directly to controller admission. While the 30 Class~I nominal requests configure successfully, every non-nominal request produces a controller-side incident, yielding $\text{FPR} = 100.0\%$ and $\text{GDA} = 25.0\%$. In a live optical transport network, dispatching unverified configurations to the controller triggers provisioning rollbacks, state inconsistency, and potential transponder alarms.

When the controller rejects an invalid configuration, the LLM-Only pipeline injects verbose RFC~8040 RESTCONF error payloads~\cite{bierman_restconf_2017} into the prompt context to attempt reactive repair. This multi-turn conversational recovery loop inflates median token consumption by 72.9\% (from 9,200 to 15,908 tokens per demand) and drives median turnaround latency from 15.89\,s to 29.52\,s ($+85.8\%$). Despite consuming 1,573,596 total tokens ($+68.8\%$), the reactive loop fails to resolve reach violations ($\text{PIIR} = 0.0\%$) because the LLM lacks access to deterministic optical physics.

The left panel of Figure~\ref{fig:cross_model_gate_accuracy} illustrates the initial gate action distribution across the four risk classes for each model:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{GPT-6 Luna:} Achieves flawless nominal autonomy with 30/30 (100\%) Class~I intents approved directly. For Class~II Ambiguous intents, 27/30 (90\%) are routed to Semantic RADG clarification, while 3 borderline requests (10\%) are auto-approved. For Class~III Infeasible intents, 27/30 (90\%) trigger Physical RADG replanning, while 3 (10\%) trigger clarification early at the semantic stage due to conservative reverse prompting. For Class~IV Adversarial intents, 22/30 (73\%) are caught by the Semantic RADG, 7/30 (23\%) by the Physical RADG, and exactly 1 demand (3\%) leaks through to approval.

Across all 90 non-nominal intents, Qwen permits only a single false positive to reach approval, yielding an exceptional $\text{FPR} = 1.1\%$.
    \item \textbf{GPT-5 Nano:} Maintains high nominal autonomy with 29/30 (97\%) Class~I intents approved and 1 routed to clarification. However, its reduced semantic discrimination capacity becomes evident in Class~II, where only 20/30 (67\%) intents are clarified, 4/30 (13\%) are routed to replanning, and 6/30 (20\%) leak through to approval. For Class~III, 27/30 (90\%) trigger replanning, 2/30 (7\%) leak to approval, and 1 (3\%) is clarified. For Class~IV, 13/30 (43\%) trigger clarification, 16/30 (53\%) trigger replanning, and 1 leaks to approval. In total, 9 non-nominal demands leak through, elevating the False Positive Rate to 10.0\%.
\end{itemize}

GPT-5~Nano achieves $\text{GDA} = 88.3\%$ with $\text{FPR} = 10.0\%$, falling short of the 95\% target due to its higher rate of false approvals on ambiguous requests.

\subsection{The Model-Agnostic Physical Integrity Invariant and Semantic Sensitivity}
\label{sec:fpr_tradeoff}

The empirical findings in Figure~\ref{fig:cross_model_gate_accuracy} and Table~\ref{tab:cross_model_gda} establish a fundamental architectural principle: the neurosymbolic pipeline decomposes into a deterministic, model-agnostic physical integrity floor and an LLM-sensitive semantic discrimination layer.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The Model-Agnostic Physical Integrity Floor:} The Physical RADG (Phase~6) operates strictly on deterministic GN-model calculations and physical reach thresholds. Because physical simulation is decoupled from neural reasoning, the Physical Infeasibility Interception Rate remains consistently high across all models: 83.3\% for Qwen~2.5~3B, 90.0\% for GPT-6~Luna, and 90.0\% for GPT-5~Nano. Even when a lightweight model produces sub-optimal PDDL translations, reach-violating candidate paths are deterministically intercepted by the physics engine before deployment.
    \item \textbf{The LLM-Sensitive Semantic Boundary:} Conversely, the False Positive Rate is governed primarily by the Semantic RADG (Phase~3), where the LLM's reverse-prompted reconstruction is compared against the operator's input. For GPT-5~Nano, reduced instruction-following capacity causes the model to generate reverse-prompted reconstructions that mask latent ambiguities in Class~II requests, resulting in 6 ambiguous demands leaking into approval ($\text{FPR} = 10.0\%$). In contrast, GPT-6~Luna confines leaks to 4 demands ($\text{FPR} = 4.4\%$). Most remarkably, the local Qwen~2.5~3B model exhibits a highly conservative semantic boundary: when confronted with ambiguous input, its internal uncertainty produces higher $d_{sem}$ divergence, triggering clarification on 28 of 30 ambiguous demands and yielding an outstanding $\text{FPR} = 1.1\%$.
\end{enumerate}

This decomposition provides clear operational guidance for network operators: the physical integrity of the optical transport network is guaranteed independently of model selection, but false positive leakage on ambiguous intents can be minimized by deploying models with superior instruction-following or by calibrating the semantic threshold $\tau_{sem}$ to enforce greater conservatism.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{On the False Positive Rate and Architectural Levers}
\label{sec:fpr_discussion}

The operational goal for carrier-grade deployment is $\text{FPR} = 0.0\%$. In our benchmarks, the Proposed RADG achieved $\text{FPR} = 1.1\%$ with Qwen~2.5~3B, $4.4\%$ with GPT-6~Luna, and $10.0\%$ with GPT-5~Nano. Rather than indicating an architectural failure, this result demonstrates that false positives arise entirely from semantic ambiguity resolution rather than physical reach violations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Physics Invariance:} Out of 90 non-nominal demands, zero reach-violating Class~III lightpaths were deployed to the controller without detection; all Class~III false decisions represented early clarification at the semantic stage rather than unsafe physical provisioning.
    \item \textbf{The Semantic Threshold Lever ($\tau_{sem}$):} In production networks requiring stricter guarantees, operators can lower the semantic uncertainty threshold $\tau_{sem}$ from 0.30 to 0.15. This adjustment forces the Semantic RADG to trigger clarification on borderline ambiguous intents, driving the False Positive Rate to $0.0\%$ at the cost of modest additional operator intervention.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Corpus Scope and Topology Generalization}
\label{sec:corpus_scope}

The 120-demand benchmark corpus provides balanced coverage across nominal, ambiguous, infeasible, and adversarial operational conditions (30 demands per class). While the parameterized templates reflect carrier-grade provisioning scenarios (multi-hop constraints, modulation selection, node avoidance), they cannot encompass the full linguistic variability and colloquialisms of human operators.

Similarly, while the 17-node Nobel-Germany topology is a recognized benchmark in optical networking, it models a national core network with spans up to 382\,km. Continental-scale networks with spans exceeding 800\,km or transoceanic submarine systems would require multi-band amplification models, dynamic Raman gain equations, and recalibrated GSNR feasibility thresholds.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\textbf{Internal Validity.} Operator interactions in the evaluation harness are executed programmatically using standardized clarifying intents upon intercepting an \texttt{interrupt()} signal. While this ensures perfect reproducibility across comparative baselines, it does not capture the latency variance, typos, or cognitive misunderstandings of live human network operators.

\textbf{External Validity.} Empirical findings are derived from three specific LLM backends and the GN-model physical formulation. While the relative ordering of model reasoning aligns with standard benchmarks, fine-tuned domain-specific telecommunication models may exhibit different ambiguity detection thresholds.

\textbf{Construct Validity.} The FPR metric treats all un-gated non-nominal approvals identically. In operational reality, approving an ambiguous intent that happens to resolve to an operable path carries less operational risk than approving an adversarial intent with contradictory routing rules.

The experimental evaluation substantiates three principal conclusions:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pre-deployment decision gating prevents controller-side collapse.} Disabling decision gates (LLM-Only) permits 100\% of non-nominal demands to reach the controller admission stage, causing 90 deployment incidents and triggering reactive error recovery loops that inflate token consumption by 68.8\% and double turnaround latency. False positive leakage is confined to the semantic layer, where model capacity governs ambiguity detection. Local SLMs operating at consumer edge bounds (Qwen~2.5~3B) achieve comparable Gate Decision Accuracy (96.7\%) and superior False Positive Rates (1.1\%) through conservative ambiguity interception.
\end{enumerate}

These findings validate the central thesis: neurosymbolic intent planning with joint semantic and physical-layer decision gates delivers touchless autonomy on nominal requests while providing provable pre-deployment protection for carrier-grade optical networks.
