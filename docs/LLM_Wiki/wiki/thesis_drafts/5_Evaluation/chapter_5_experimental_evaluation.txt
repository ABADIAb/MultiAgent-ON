\chapter{Experimental Evaluation and Results}
\label{chap:evaluation}

This chapter evaluates the Risk-Adaptive Decision Gate (RADG) architecture introduced in Chapter~\ref{chap:system_model} and implemented in Chapter~\ref{chap:implementation}. The evaluation quantifies the system's ability to intercept physically infeasible or semantically ambiguous intents \emph{before} they reach the SDON controller, while minimizing unnecessary operator interventions. Three baseline architectures are compared across a four-pillar validation framework using three LLM backends of varying capacity. Section~\ref{sec:experimental_setup} describes the testbed, corpus, and baselines. Section~\ref{sec:intra_model} presents an in-depth intra-model comparative analysis using a representative cloud-hosted LLM. Section~\ref{sec:cross_model} examines the sensitivity of gate decisions to the underlying LLM backend. Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses limitations and threats to validity, and Section~\ref{sec:eval_summary} consolidates the findings.

\section{Experimental Setup}
\label{sec:experimental_setup}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{Network Topology and Physical Parameters}
\label{sec:topology}

All experiments use the Nobel-Germany reference topology, a 17-node, 26-bidirectional-link backbone derived from the SNDlib repository~\cite{sndlib}. Each link models a deployed fiber span with heterogeneous lengths ranging from 37.5\,km to 381.9\,km. The physical layer is parameterized for ITU-T G.652 single-mode fiber (SMF-28) with attenuation coefficient $\alpha = 0.2$\,dB/km, nonlinear coefficient $\gamma = 1.3$\,W$^{-1}$km$^{-1}$, dispersion parameter $D = 16.7$\,ps/(nm$\cdot$km), and reference bandwidth $B_{ref} = 12.5$\,GHz. Inline amplifiers (ILAs) are placed every 80\,km with a noise figure $\text{NF} = 5.5$\,dB, and booster amplifiers compensate for ROADM insertion losses at each node. The GN-model QoT engine, described in Section~\ref{sec:physical_engine}, computes per-path GSNR values and receiver power levels against a threshold of $\text{GSNR}_{th} = 12.6$\,dB and minimum receiver sensitivity $P_{rx,min} = -28$\,dBm.

\subsection{LLM Backends Under Evaluation}
\label{sec:llm_backends}

The pipeline is evaluated with three LLM backends that span the capability and deployment spectrum:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Qwen 2.5 3B} (\texttt{qwen2.5:3b}, Ollama, local inference): A 3-billion-parameter open-weights model running on consumer hardware (NVIDIA RTX~3050, 4\,GB VRAM). Represents the lower bound of model capacity available for edge or air-gapped deployments.
    \item \textbf{GPT-6 Luna} (\texttt{gpt-6-luna}, OpenAI API): A cloud-hosted commercial model representing the current state of the art in instruction following and structured output generation.
    \item \textbf{GPT-5 Nano} (\texttt{gpt-5-nano-2025-08-07}, OpenAI API): A compact cloud-hosted model positioned between the two extremes, offering faster inference at reduced reasoning depth.
\end{itemize}

This selection tests whether the neurosymbolic architecture maintains its integrity guarantees independently of the neural component's capacity, or whether gate accuracy degrades as a function of LLM quality.

\subsection{Benchmark Corpus and Risk Classes}
\label{sec:corpus}

The evaluation corpus consists of 120 operator intent demands partitioned equally into four risk classes of 30 demands each. Each class targets a distinct failure mode of the pipeline:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Class~I (Nominal):} Unambiguous, physically feasible intents with complete constraint specification (e.g., ``Route 100\,Gbps from Hamburg to Munich avoiding Frankfurt''). Expected outcome: autonomous approval with zero HITL interrupts.
    \item \textbf{Class~II (Ambiguous):} Intents with deliberately missing or contradictory constraints that increase semantic uncertainty $U_{sem}$ above the threshold $\tau_{sem}$. Expected outcome: the Semantic RADG triggers a Clarify interrupt via reverse prompting.
    \item \textbf{Class~III (Infeasible):} Intents with valid semantic content but physically impossible routing requirements (e.g., demanding 25\,dB GSNR on a 381\,km single-span link). Expected outcome: the Physical RADG triggers a Replan interrupt after GN-model evaluation confirms $\text{QoT}_{valid} = 0$.
    \item \textbf{Class~IV (Adversarial):} Intents combining both semantic ambiguity and physical infeasibility. Expected outcome: the pipeline intercepts at the earliest applicable gate.
\end{itemize}

The Qwen 2.5 3B model uses a 20-demand compact variant of this corpus (5 per class) due to its local inference constraints; GPT-6~Luna and GPT-5~Nano use the full 120-demand corpus.

\subsection{Baseline Architectures}
\label{sec:baselines}

Three baseline architectures are compared. Each shares the same LangGraph pipeline and physical-layer engine; only the gating and HITL engagement policy differs:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Proposed RADG (V5):} The full architecture with dual sequential gates. The Semantic RADG evaluates $U_{sem}$ and the Physical RADG evaluates $\text{QoT}_{valid}$. HITL interrupts are triggered selectively, proportional to assessed risk.
    \item \textbf{Always-On HITL:} An ablation where every demand, regardless of risk class, is forced through a mandatory HITL review step after the semantic gate. This baseline isolates the cost of indiscriminate human engagement.
    \item \textbf{LLM-Only:} An ablation where both decision gates are disabled. Every demand is forwarded directly from PDDL translation to path synthesis and QoT validation, with no pre-deployment interception of semantically ambiguous or physically infeasible intents. This baseline models the behavior of a conventional LLM-based planner without neurosymbolic gating.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Performance Metrics: The Four Validation Pillars}
\label{sec:metrics}

The evaluation framework is organized into four validation pillars, each targeting a distinct operational concern:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Pillar~1 --- Semantic Translation Accuracy:} Measures the fidelity of the NL-to-PDDL translation.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Constraint Retention Rate: $\text{CRR} = N_{preserved} / N_{explicit}$.
        \item CFG AST Pass Rate: fraction of PDDL outputs that pass the context-free grammar validator ($v_{struct} \in \{0, 1\}$).
        \item Semantic Agreement: $1 - d_{sem}$, the concordance between the original intent and the reverse-prompted reconstruction.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Pillar~2 --- Physical Feasibility and Integrity:} Measures the system's ability to prevent physically infeasible plans from reaching the controller.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item False Positive Rate: $\text{FPR} = N_{risky\_approved} / N_{total}$. Target: $0.0\%$.
        \item Physical Infeasibility Interception Rate: $\text{PIIR} = N_{class3\_replanned} / N_{class3\_total}$.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Pillar~3 --- Efficiency and Operator Friction:} Measures resource consumption and human workload.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item End-to-end latency $T_{E2E}$ (median and mean, in seconds).
        \item Token footprint $T_{tokens}$ (median and mean, per demand).
        \item Mean HITL interventions per demand $\bar{N}_{hitl}$.
        \item Task Completion Rate (TCR): fraction of demands resolved without timeout.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Pillar~4 --- Gate Reliability and Autonomy:} Measures the accuracy and selectivity of the gating mechanism.
    \begin{itemize}
        \item Gate Decision Accuracy: $\text{GDA} = N_{correct} / N_{total}$.
        \item Selective HITL Precision: fraction of HITL interrupts that were directed at genuinely non-nominal intents.
    \end{itemize}
\end{enumerate}

The next section presents a detailed intra-model analysis using GPT-6~Luna as the representative backend, illustrating the three operational dilemmas that motivate the RADG architecture.

\subsection{The FPR--Model Quality Tradeoff}
\label{sec:fpr_tradeoff}

The FPR values across backends---0.0\% (Qwen), 3.3\% (GPT-6~Luna), and 16.7\% (GPT-5~Nano)---reveal a structural property of the architecture. False positives originate exclusively at the Semantic RADG (Phase~3), where the LLM's reverse-prompted reconstruction fails to flag an ambiguous or adversarial intent. The Physical RADG (Phase~6) operates on deterministic GN-model outputs and is independent of the LLM backend: its interception rate (PIIR) remains consistently high across all models (83.3\%--90.0\%).

This decomposition has two implications. First, the Physical RADG provides a model-agnostic deterministic integrity floor: regardless of LLM quality, physically infeasible lightpaths are intercepted by the GN-model engine. Second, the FPR is a direct function of the LLM's semantic discrimination capacity. GPT-5~Nano's 16.7\% FPR traces to 15 demands---predominantly Class~IV adversarial intents with deliberately obfuscated constraint contradictions---where the model produced reverse-prompted reconstructions that appeared semantically consistent despite containing latent conflicts. GPT-6~Luna reduced this count to 3 demands, and Qwen~2.5~3B achieved zero false positives by generating conservatively high $U_{sem}$ values that triggered clarification on borderline cases.

This finding provides operational guidance for production deployments: the RADG architecture maintains its physical integrity guarantee regardless of backend selection, but FPR can be reduced by selecting an LLM with stronger instruction-following and semantic disambiguation capabilities, or by lowering the semantic threshold $\tau_{sem}$ at the cost of increased HITL friction.

\section{Discussion and Limitations}
\label{sec:discussion}
% ============================================================================

\subsection{On the FPR $\neq$ 0\% Observation}
\label{sec:fpr_discussion}

The initial target of $\text{FPR} = 0.0\%$ is met only by Qwen~2.5~3B on the compact 20-demand corpus. GPT-6~Luna achieves $\text{FPR} = 3.3\%$ (3 false positives out of 120 demands) and GPT-5~Nano reaches $\text{FPR} = 16.7\%$ (15 false positives). This outcome does not invalidate the architecture; rather, it decomposes the integrity guarantee into two layers:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The deterministic layer (Physical RADG):} Operates on GN-model outputs with zero dependence on the LLM. Its interception rate (PIIR: 83.3\%--90.0\%) is bounded by the physics engine's coverage of the demand space, not by neural quality.
    \item \textbf{The neural layer (Semantic RADG):} Depends on the LLM's ability to produce accurate reverse-prompted reconstructions. False positives occur when the LLM generates a reconstruction that appears semantically consistent with the original intent despite containing latent constraint violations.
\end{enumerate}

This decomposition provides a clear operational lever: deploying a higher-capability LLM reduces FPR without requiring any architectural changes. Alternatively, lowering $\tau_{sem}$ increases the Semantic RADG's sensitivity at the cost of additional HITL interventions on borderline cases.

\subsection{Corpus Scope and Generalization}
\label{sec:corpus_scope}

The benchmark corpus is synthetic, generated from parameterized templates that cover the four risk classes. While the templates model realistic operator requests (multi-hop routing with GSNR constraints, node avoidance, bandwidth specifications), they do not capture the full linguistic diversity of production network operations centers. The 120-demand full corpus provides statistical coverage for per-class analysis (30 samples per class), but the compact 20-demand corpus used for Qwen~2.5~3B (5 per class) limits the confidence of per-class breakdowns for that model.

The Nobel-Germany 17-node topology, while standard in optical networking research, represents a single national backbone. Generalization to continental-scale or submarine topologies with different fiber parameters would require recalibration of the GN-model constants and re-evaluation of the GSNR threshold.

\subsection{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

\textbf{Internal validity.} The HITL interactions are simulated programmatically: the evaluation harness provides deterministic follow-up responses when the pipeline triggers an \texttt{interrupt()} call. This eliminates variability from human response time and content quality, but does not capture the cognitive load or error rate of real operators.

\textbf{External validity.} Results are specific to the three evaluated LLM backends and the Nobel-Germany topology. The relative ordering of model performance (GPT-6~Luna $>$ Qwen~2.5~3B $>$ GPT-5~Nano on GDA) may not hold for other model families or fine-tuned variants.

\textbf{Construct validity.} The FPR metric treats all false positives equally, regardless of the severity of the underlying physical infeasibility. A demand that is borderline-feasible (GSNR within 0.3\,dB of the threshold) is penalized identically to one that is grossly infeasible.

The experimental results support three principal findings:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{The RADG architecture eliminates controller-side deployment failures.} Across all three LLM backends, the LLM-Only baseline produces $\text{FPR} = 100\%$ and $\text{PIIR} = 0.0\%$, forwarding every non-nominal demand directly to the SDON controller. The Proposed RADG intercepts 72\%--75\% of all demands at the pre-deployment stage, reducing wasted compute by 40\%--66\% in token consumption and preventing between 75 and 90 controller incidents per evaluation run.

    \item \textbf{Selective HITL engagement reduces operator friction without degrading integrity.} The Always-On HITL baseline wastes 25\%--29\% of its interrupts on nominal traffic that requires no human attention, degrading Selective HITL Precision to 71.4\%--74.4\%. The Proposed RADG achieves 97.8\%--100\% Selective HITL Precision by directing interrupts exclusively at semantically ambiguous or physically infeasible demands.

    \item \textbf{FPR is a function of LLM semantic quality, not of the gate architecture.} The Physical RADG provides a model-agnostic deterministic integrity floor (PIIR: 83.3\%--90.0\%). All false positives originate at the Semantic RADG, where the LLM's reverse-prompted reconstruction fails to flag latent constraint violations. This decomposition provides clear operational guidance: upgrading the LLM backend reduces FPR without architectural changes.
\end{enumerate}

These findings validate the core hypothesis of the thesis: a pre-deployment, risk-adaptive gating mechanism based on joint semantic and physical-layer assessment reduces operator friction and prevents physically infeasible configurations from reaching the network controller. Chapter~\ref{chap:conclusion} synthesizes these results into the broader contributions of the thesis and identifies directions for future work.
