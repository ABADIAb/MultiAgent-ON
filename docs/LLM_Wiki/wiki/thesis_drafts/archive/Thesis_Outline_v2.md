# **1 Introduction** 
- **1.1 Overview of Intent-Based Optical Networks:** The shift towards Software-Defined Optical Networks (SDON) and the growing role of Generative AI in interpreting high-level operator intents. 
- **1.2 Motivation:** The critical bottlenecks in translating human intent into physical configurations (Token Budget Saturation and Hallucinated Physics). 
- **1.3 Thesis Outline:** Brief description of the remaining chapters. 

# **2 Background and State of the Art** 
- **2.1 Autonomous Optical Networks and Multi-Agent Systems:** Review of existing SOTA solutions (Confucius, AutoLight) and their focus on scaling multi-agent orchestration. 
- **2.2 Limitations of Purely Generative Pipelines:** Why LLMs inherently struggle with deterministic Quality of Transmission (QoT) constraints and large topological datasets. 
- **2.3 The Research Gap:** Emphasizing the lack of pre-deployment, fail-fast evaluation mechanisms in current literature, which mostly relies on fixed retry loops after a deployment failure. 

# **3 System Model: The Risk-Adaptive Neurosymbolic Architecture** 
- **3.1 Problem Statement:** A formal definition of the five bottlenecks (Token Saturation, Hallucinated Physics, Semantic Drift, Post-Deployment Failure, and Suboptimal HITL Engagement) and the specific research gap our architecture addresses.
- **3.2 Conceptual Framework:** Introduction of the Risk-Adaptive Neurosymbolic Intent Planning system as the core solution. 
- **3.3 Strict Neurosymbolic Separation:** The design principle of restricting the LLM to parsing semantics and translating intent into formal PDDL constraints, while a non-neural symbolic solver handles physical routing. 
- **3.4 The Risk-Adaptive Decision Gate (RADG):** The pre-deployment pipeline that sequentially evaluates semantic uncertainty ($U_{sem}$) and physical-layer QoT risk ($R_{qot}$). 
- **3.5 Formal Human-In-The-Loop (HITL) via Reverse Prompting:** How the system bounds operator approvals to exact logical constraints when clarification is required. 

# **4 Neurosymbolic Pipeline Implementation** 
- **4.1 Network State and Knowledge Graph (GraphRAG):** The implementation of GraphRAG to provide localized network context to the LLM, solving the token budget bottleneck. 
- **4.2 Semantic and QoT Validation Modules:** The mechanics of the threshold-based decision functions, using embedding similarity for semantic risk and a deterministic GN-model for physical parameter validation. 
- **4.3 Decision Outcomes:** The three workflow outcomes generated by the RADG: Auto-Approve (Safe + Clear), Clarify (Ambiguous), and Suggest Replan (Infeasible QoT). 

# **5 Experimental Evaluation and Results** 
- **5.1 Experimental Setup and Baselines:** Definition of the synthetic intent dataset and the benchmark strategies (No-HITL, Always-HITL, and Fixed-Retry approaches).
- **5.2 Performance Metrics:** The metrics used to quantify efficacy: Unsafe Approval Rate (UAR), Human Interaction Count (HIC), QoT Feasibility Rate (QFR), End-to-End Latency (E2EL), and Token Cost (TC). 
- **5.3 Performance under Safe Conditions:** Results of the architecture autonomously processing unambiguous, QoT-valid intents. 
- **5.4 Performance under Ambiguity:** Analysis of the system engaging the HITL proportionally via reverse prompting when constraints are missing. 
- **5.5 Comparison against Baselines:** Numerical evidence showing how the Risk-Adaptive architecture outperforms No-HITL (safety) and Always-HITL (efficiency). 
- **5.6 Summary of Findings:** The computational and operational savings achieved by catching ambiguity and physical infeasibility before deployment. 

# **6 Conclusion and Future Work** 
- **6.1 Summary of Contributions:** The value of merging LLM semantic reasoning with formal PDDL constraint solving via the Risk-Adaptive Decision Gate. 
- **6.2 Future Work:** Expanding the simplified Python symbolic solver and mock GraphRAG into production-grade network orchestrators.
